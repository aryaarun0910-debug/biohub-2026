# Learned Association, MOT/GNN, Optimal Transport, and Test-Time Adaptation

**Date: 2026-07-12**
**Lane:** `03_association_mot_ot_tta.md` — maps to the three-layer win architecture's linking/association layer, which prior analysis (`02`/leaderboard reframe) identified as the dominant headroom (~+0.18 available from perfect linking on frozen detections) and where Trackastra zero-shot already failed as a wholesale hidden-embryo replacement (0.865 public vs 0.889 baseline).

---

## Executive summary: 5 highest-value transferable methods

1. **Reverse-validation-style gradient-free hyperparameter/consistency scoring on the hidden movie** (generalizes the "Path Consistency" idea from Lu et al., CVPR 2024, and forward-backward cycle-consistency) — use unlabeled-video self-consistency as a *proxy metric* to select/gate linking hyperparameters or blend weights, never as a loss that touches model weights first. This is the single safest, cheapest way to spend the automated-self-supervision allowance the rules grant on the hidden target movie. **[Target adaptation, gradient-free]**

2. **Unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) / Sinkhorn-with-dustbin assignment** (SuperGlue's OT layer; Sinkhorn for constant-velocity agents, arXiv:1907.05036; UOT for particle tracking, arXiv:2407.04583; UOT for multi-camera tracking, Springer 2021) as a deterministic drop-in replacement for the greedy/Hungarian cost-matrix solve. Explicitly models births/deaths/divisions via slack mass instead of forcing brittle 1:1 matches — directly relevant since divisions and volume-boundary entry/exit are exactly the edge cases the metric penalizes. **[OT hedge]**

3. **GNN message-passing edge-classification over a candidate graph** (Braso & Leal-Taixe, "Learning a Neural Solver for MOT," CVPR 2020 oral, github.com/dvl-tum/mot_neural_solver; "GNN for Cell Tracking in Microscopy Videos," ECCV 2022, arXiv:2202.04731) used as a *selective repair* layer that only overrides the classical linker on edges where it disagrees with a transformer/learned scorer, rather than replacing the whole pipeline (the failure mode that hurt Trackastra). **[Selective repair]**

4. **DARTH-style holistic test-time adaptation with EMA teacher + confidence gating** (Segu, Schiele, Yu, ICCV 2023, arXiv:2310.01926, github.com/mattiasegu/darth) — the most complete published recipe for *safely* adapting a tracker at test time without labels: detection-consistency + patch-contrastive losses, teacher-student EMA, high-confidence-only pseudo-targets. Provides the template for what NOT to do naively (their own ablation shows patch-contrastive loss alone collapses detection quality) and what minimal safe subset (affine/embedding-head-only, few steps, reverse-fold-gated) we should port. **[Target adaptation, last-layer]**

5. **TENT's entropy-minimization failure mode as an explicit hazard list** (Wang et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv:2006.10726; DARTH's own ablation shows "Tent fails... entropy minimization harms localization") — not a method to adopt, but the single most important negative result to respect: any TTA scheme that only minimizes prediction confidence/entropy on the hidden movie is likely to overfit to trivial or degenerate matches (e.g., collapsing everything onto the nearest neighbor) rather than genuinely correcting distribution shift. Every TTA experiment we run must include an explicit anti-collapse gate. **[Metric-aware selection / guardrail]**

## Deep dive: the safest first target-time-adaptation experiment

### Why gradient-free-first, not DARTH/TENT-style weight updates

Every TTA method surveyed above that updates weights (DARTH, TENT, EATA, mean-teacher SFOD) needs *some* held-out signal to know whether the adaptation helped or hurt — and by construction we cannot get that signal on the actual hidden private-test embryos (no labels, one shot). DARTH's own ablation shows that even a carefully engineered two-loss, EMA-teacher, confidence-gated scheme can produce a *smaller* gain or even a regression in the harder generalization direction (DanceTrack→MOT17 gains were much smaller than MOT17→DanceTrack — direction-dependent, unpredictable magnitude). TENT can fail outright under severe shift. Given we get exactly one attempt on the private embryos with no ability to check the outcome, **any weight-updating scheme is a one-shot bet with unknown variance** — precisely the risk profile we should avoid for the primary weapon.

The safer alternative: use the self-supervised *signals* from this literature (path consistency, cycle consistency, detection-consistency-under-jitter) purely as **scoring functions** to pick among a small set of pre-built, already-validated candidate configurations (e.g., "classical distance-cost linker" vs "OT/Sinkhorn-with-dustbin linker" vs "Trackastra-blended linker" vs a couple of hyperparameter settings for each). No weights change. The only thing that changes per-hidden-embryo is *which already-validated candidate gets selected*, and by how much a candidate's parameters are nudged within a pre-registered safe range (e.g., a single distance-threshold scalar, or the OT unbalanced-mass penalty). This turns "test-time adaptation" into "test-time selection/calibration," which is far lower-variance.

### The experiment

**Step 0 — build the candidate pool (done ahead of time, on labeled data only).**
Construct 3-5 candidate linking configurations from the OT-hedge and selective-repair methods above (e.g., classical nearest-neighbor/Hungarian baseline; Sinkhorn+dustbin; unbalanced-OT with 2-3 mass-penalty settings; Trackastra-agreement-blended). Each is fully specified and frozen — no per-target learning inside a candidate.

**Step 1 — define the unsupervised scoring signal.**
For a given hidden movie and a given candidate linking, compute, using only the candidate's own output (no labels):
- *Cycle-consistency rate*: fraction of links that are mutually nearest under forward and backward association.
- *Path-consistency score*: KL-divergence (lower is better) between association distributions computed by skipping different subsets of intermediate frames (à la Lu et al. — reuse their formulation, applied to whatever base cost/probability the candidate produces, not to a newly trained network).
- Combine into a single scalar (e.g., a weighted sum, or simply rank candidates on cycle-consistency and use path-consistency as a tiebreaker) — pre-register the combination rule so it isn't tuned post-hoc on the very data it's being validated against.

**Step 2 — simulate the hidden-test decision via reverse-fold validation.**
This is the crux of making the experiment safe. We only have 2 labeled embryos (44b6, 6bba). Simulate the real deployment twice:
- *Fold A*: treat 6bba as if it were the hidden embryo. Run Step 1's scoring procedure on 6bba **using no labels** (blind the code path to 6bba's ground truth) to pick a candidate/hyperparameter. Then, and only then, reveal 6bba's real labels and compute the actual edge-jaccard-based metric for the scoring-selected candidate vs. the metric for the fixed frozen baseline (the "do nothing" default).
- *Fold B*: same procedure with roles reversed (44b6 is the simulated hidden embryo, 6bba is used to build the candidate pool / any priors).
- Record, per fold: Δmetric = metric(scoring-selected candidate) − metric(frozen baseline).

**Step 3 — the min-fold gate.**
Only enable target-time selection on the real hidden embryos if **both** folds show Δmetric ≥ a pre-registered small positive threshold (e.g., +0.003-0.005 on the weighted adjusted-edge-jaccard scale — small because we have only 2 folds and want to avoid deploying on noise) **and** neither fold regresses below −ε (e.g., −0.001) even in the worse-performing direction. This directly mirrors DARTH's own finding that gains are highly direction-dependent (their MOT17→DanceTrack gain was far larger than DanceTrack→MOT17) — we should expect the same asymmetry between our two embryos and must not deploy unless the *worse* direction still clears the bar. If either fold fails the gate, fall back to the frozen baseline for the private submission — this fallback must be the literal default codepath, not a fix-it-later branch.

**Step 4 (optional, only if Step 2/3 pass with margin) — last-layer-only calibration.**
If and only if the gradient-free selection passes its gate with comfortable margin, a strictly limited follow-on experiment may fine-tune *only* an affine re-scaling of the final association-score logits (analogous to TENT's BN-affine-only scope, or DARTH's embedding head in isolation) using the same self-supervised consistency signal as the loss, capped at a small fixed number of steps, with the EMA-teacher/confidence-gating pattern from DARTH to prevent collapse. This must be re-validated with the identical reverse-fold + min-fold gate as Steps 2-3 before it can replace Step 1-3's pure-selection approach. Given the one-shot nature of the private test and the small (2-embryo) validation budget, we recommend treating Step 4 as a stretch goal, not the primary plan — the literature (TENT collapse, DARTH's direction-asymmetric gains) suggests weight-updating TTA has a real chance of being *worse than doing nothing* on an unlucky domain shift, whereas pure candidate-selection from a pre-validated pool cannot underperform the frozen baseline by more than the gate's ε.

**Cost:** Step 0-3 requires no GPU training — only inference with existing/frozen models plus classical graph algorithms (Sinkhorn, cycle-consistency counting), all well within the 12h offline budget. Step 4, if attempted, is a small number of fine-tuning steps on a low-dimensional affine layer, also cheap.

## What likely fails to transfer, and why

Trackastra's zero-shot transformer already demonstrated the central failure mode: it helped on single crops but *regressed* (0.865 vs 0.889) as a wholesale hidden-embryo replacement. The literature here explains why, and what would need to change:

- **Too few, too homogeneous training identities.** Trackastra (and any learned pairwise/global association transformer — MOTR, MOTRv2, GTR) learns statistics of *this specific* training distribution (feature scale, intensity range, division rate, local density, frame-rate/dt). With only 2 labeled embryos to fine-tune or validate against, a transformer's attention weights and score calibration are fit to those 2 embryos' particular imaging conditions and cannot be expected to generalize to a disjoint, unseen embryo's different density/illumination/velocity regime — this is a small-N generalization problem, not a modeling problem, and no architecture change fixes it alone.
- **Greedy linking amplifies miscalibration.** Trackastra's own design (simple greedy linking over learned pairwise scores) works because the paper's benchmarks show its *scores* are well-calibrated on distributions similar to training. Under domain shift, the same greedy linker commits early to locally-plausible but globally-wrong links, with no OT-style slack/dustbin to absorb uncertainty — precisely why an unbalanced-OT or dustbin-based assignment layer (SuperGlue, UOT) that explicitly tolerates non-matches would be more robust than greedy commitment.
- **End-to-end learned trackers need scale.** MOTR/MOTRv2/GTR are trained on orders-of-magnitude more diverse video (MOT17, DanceTrack, TAO, BDD100K) than our 2-embryo budget can supply; their track-query/trajectory-query mechanisms are data-hungry and there is no published evidence of cell/microscopy cross-domain transfer for them at all — these are useful as *conceptual* references (the value of long-window global association, of decoupling detection from association) but not as drop-in models.
- **Appearance-based features are the least transferable axis.** Cross-embryo shifts plausibly change absolute intensity, marker expression, and density much more than local relative geometry does. EmbedTrack's success as a CTC generalist (top-3 on 7/9 very different datasets) using simple offset/bandwidth embeddings — not deep attention over appearance — is evidence that *simpler, more physical* features (relative position, local kNN structure, velocity coherence) generalize better than learned appearance embeddings across CTC-style heterogeneous datasets. This directly argues for the OT/FGW/CPD feature-design entries above (matching relative geometry/structure rather than absolute coordinates or intensities) as the more transferable axis, and for treating Trackastra as a *contributor of one more edge score to blend*, never as the sole link.
- **What WOULD make a learned association method transfer:** (1) training across many diverse embryos/imaging conditions rather than 2 (not available to us); (2) explicit density/velocity-normalization in the feature design (e.g., normalize relative distances by local median inter-nucleus spacing, normalize velocities by local median speed) so absolute density/speed differences between embryos don't shift the input distribution the model sees; (3) an explicit no-match/dustbin/unbalanced-mass mechanism so the model isn't forced into a wrong match when uncertain; (4) validation via reverse-fold (as above) before trusting any single learned model as a wholesale replacement, using it instead as one signal in an ensemble/selective-repair scheme; (5) calibration (not full retraining) at target time, since calibration/threshold shifts are lower-variance to adapt than representations.

## Open questions

1. **Signal quality on sparse, visually near-identical nuclei.** Path-consistency and cycle-consistency were validated on pedestrian/vehicle MOT where appearance is at least somewhat discriminative. Zebrafish nuclei are far more visually homogeneous (worse aperture/correspondence problem) — does the consistency signal remain informative enough to rank candidates reliably, or does it degenerate toward uniform low-confidence everywhere, making Step 1-3 above uninformative rather than actively harmful? This should be checked empirically on the reverse-fold before trusting the gate.
2. **Do OT/UOT hyperparameters (mass-creation penalty, entropy regularization) need per-embryo retuning to be effective, which would defeat the "no target-time learning" safety property?** If the same fixed UOT settings validated on 44b6/6bba don't transfer their *hyperparameters* (only their formulation) to a differently-dense hidden embryo, we may need Step 1-3's selection procedure to also select among a small grid of UOT hyperparameter settings, which is still gradient-free but enlarges the candidate pool and the multiple-comparisons risk on only 2 validation folds.
3. **Compute/time budget for full-movie unsupervised scoring.** Path-consistency-style scoring over many frame-skip combinations across a full 3D+time hidden movie could be nontrivially expensive; needs a cost estimate against the 12h offline ceiling before committing to it as the primary plan, and a fallback to a cheaper subset (e.g., cycle-consistency only, or scoring on a random subsample of frame pairs).
4. **Out-of-distribution gating.** Is there a cheap, pre-computable statistic (density, velocity spread, division rate estimated from frozen detections alone) that would let us detect *before* running any adaptation whether the hidden embryo is similar enough to 44b6/6bba that the frozen baseline is already safe, versus dissimilar enough that the selection procedure's Step 2-3 gate is likely to matter? This would let us skip the extra compute/risk entirely on "easy" hidden embryos.
5. **Ensemble vs. replacement.** Given Trackastra helped on single crops but not wholesale, would a confidence-weighted blend (Trackastra score + classical distance cost + OT/UOT cost, combined via the GNN-edge-classifier or Ultrack-style multi-hypothesis ILP) recover the single-crop benefit without the wholesale regression? This is the most promising unexplored combination from this survey and deserves a dedicated experiment before the competition deadline, independent of the TTA question.
