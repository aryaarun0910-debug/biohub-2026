Dataset EDA & Fold-0 Prediction Results Biohub Cell Tracking dataset (199 videos) + our B (downsample, Gaussian-CenterNet) checkpoint, det_threshold=0.2, global ILP linking

This report has two parts: Part I is a deep exploratory data analysis (EDA) of all 199 training videos in data/dense_channel (metadata only — voxel scale, annotation density, division frequency, intensity statistics), and Part II evaluates how our trained B checkpoint (TemporalUNet3D + SimpleNodeTransformer, Gaussian-CenterNet detection loss, trained on strided downsample=(1,4,4) frames) performs against the dataset, using the standard evaluation recipe (5 fold-0 test videos, full frames, det_threshold=0.2, global ILP linking with the host's ILP weights).

1Dataset overview

199 training videos, each a 4D volume (T, Z, Y, X), most spanning T≈100 frames at (Z=64, Y=256, X=256) voxels, with anisotropic physical scale ≈ (1.625, 0.406, 0.406) µm/voxel (Z, Y, X). The ground truth is sparse: only a small subset of the true cells in each video are manually annotated with track IDs; the GEFF metadata additionally carries an estimated_number_of_nodes extra — the estimated TRUE total cell count for that video, used by the competition metric's over-detection penalty.

Key finding: 56.3% of the 199 training videos have zero annotated divisions. This is a strong prior for why division_jaccard tends to sit at 0 in our evaluations — it isn't necessarily a pure model failure; there is often very little (or no) division signal available in a given video to learn from or to be scored against in the first place. Any future work on division detection should prioritise identifying and up-weighting the minority of videos that actually contain divisions.

4Sparsity: annotated vs. estimated true cell count

The estimated true cell count per video (estimated_number_of_nodes) ranges from 3783 to 78644 (mean 23744), while only a small fraction of those cells are ever annotated:

Distribution of the estimated TRUE cell count per video.
Distribution of annotation sparsity (annotated / estimated total) per video — mean 6.1%, median 3.6%.
Why this matters for training: with only ~4% of true cells annotated (median), a detector that is only ever supervised on annotated positions and never sees explicit negatives will never learn what unannotated background looks like. This is exactly why negative-patch sampling (neg_patch_ratio) and masking the linking loss to annotated rows/columns only (see the training-methodology report) are necessary, not optional.

8What the numbers tell us

1. Detection recall is strong (0.81–1.0 per video, 0.927 aggregate). The Gaussian-CenterNet detector at det_threshold=0.2 reliably finds almost all annotated cells; the weakest video (267148e4, recall 0.868) is also the one with the most annotated edges (277) and by far the most edge false negatives (106) — likely a denser, harder region of the video rather than a systematic detector issue.
2. Predicted node counts are consistently well below the estimated TRUE total. Across all 5 videos, our prediction (after NMS + ILP) detects tens of thousands of cells — close to, but under, the estimated true total. This means the over-detection penalty in the competition metric is not being triggered; the model is not spuriously firing detections everywhere.
3. Division Jaccard is 0 for every video in this fold, consistent with the dataset-wide prior (56% of all videos have zero annotated divisions). Given that divisions are this rare in the ground truth, the marginal value of aggressively optimising the detector/linker for division recall specifically (rather than incidentally, via the ILP's division_weight) is limited unless training deliberately oversamples the minority of division-containing videos.
